# Initial launch and growth plan

Start with a controlled Malta and Gozo pilot that validates supply, request quality, and safe fulfilment. Treat each phase as an operating experiment; do not advertise service coverage or response times that the current worker records cannot support.

## Recruit the first workers

1. Define a small initial set of eligible categories and localities from actual worker interest. Keep regulated work hidden until the verification process and evidence review are operational.
2. Recruit through direct outreach to local tradespeople, community groups, vocational contacts, and referrals. Explain that this is an early marketplace, how requests and simulated payments work, and what information workers will share.
3. Offer a simple worker-assisted setup: a team member can help enter the profile, categories, service modes, price model, service radius, and weekly availability. Let the worker review and submit their own profile and verification evidence. Never imply self-reported details have been verified.
4. Contact new workers manually to confirm their service area, response expectations, and ability to accept real enquiries. Start with a small manageable roster and pause promotion where requests cannot be served.
5. Collect consent before using profile photos, biographies, or portfolio material. Give workers a clear way to correct or remove non-transactional profile data.

## Share useful links

Give each worker a stable profile link they can share in WhatsApp, social profiles, business cards, or email. Share links to a real service category only where the page explains that service and shows actual, current providers. Provide a concise copyable message with the worker's service area and a reminder that availability is subject to confirmation. A worker link should never promise a booking before the worker accepts it.

Ask early customers for permission before using a testimonial. Reviews must follow completed, legitimate jobs and remain attached to the real transaction; do not seed or solicit fake ratings. Track referral source with an optional non-sensitive campaign tag rather than collecting extra personal data.

## Earn local search visibility

Build useful category pages with original explanations, eligibility/safety notes, supported modes, and genuine local provider listings when available. Add locality detail only when there is enough real information to help someone choose a service; avoid publishing near-duplicate pages for every Malta locality. Keep canonical URLs, sitemap and robots responses accurate, and remove or noindex empty pages. Use structured data only for entities and facts the page actually displays.

Use plain English first. Add Maltese-language content when a fluent reviewer can maintain it. Keep service descriptions clear about quote-required jobs, simulated payment, and which professions require approved credentials. Do not create doorway pages, keyword-stuffed copy, fabricated availability, or fake local addresses.

## Learn from a small set of metrics

Review weekly counts for worker signups, completed profiles, verification submissions and decisions, customer signups, valid requests, worker response, accepted/confirmed/completed/cancelled bookings, and review submission. Separate demo/test records from real pilot activity. Use denominators and date ranges, and do not present gross booking values as revenue. The admin figures are simulated until a licensed payment provider and accounting process are introduced.

For each drop-off, combine aggregate metrics with opt-in conversations. Avoid collecting sensitive free-text details unless necessary to fulfil the job. Set a retention period and access limits for support notes. Early priorities are profile completeness, fit between demand and worker coverage, time to first response, completion rate, cancellation reason, and safety escalations.

## Worker-assisted onboarding

A coordinator can invite workers to a short setup session, help them complete their own account, and explain how availability timestamps and verification states appear. Record only the worker's requested follow-up and consent. Never ask for passwords, enter private credentials on a worker's behalf, or mark a worker verified without evidence review. Use the same documented checklist for all participants and record process gaps for product improvements.

This approach helps the first cohort get listed without suggesting the product has automated vetting or broad live coverage. Expand categories and localities only when support and verification capacity can keep up.
