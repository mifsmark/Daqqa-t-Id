## Worker-assisted setup checklist

Use the app’s three profile steps as the guide. The worker controls their account and reviews everything before saving.

1. **Explain the pilot.** It is small, manually supported, may have few or no requests, and does not guarantee work or income. Explain what data becomes public and the correction/pause/removal route.
2. **Account and details.** Have the worker create the account and password privately. Ask them to choose their display name and service locality. Never ask for, view, record, or type their password.
3. **Scope and services.** Select only services and working modes the worker offers. For this pilot use only ordinary cleaning or assembly/setup tasks. Agree exclusions such as hazardous chemicals, heavy lifting, or power-tool use in participatory modes; keep regulated trades out of scope.
4. **Travel, pricing, and description.** Worker chooses realistic radius, hourly/fixed/quote pricing, and optional description. Ask them to check the amount and whether materials/travel are included. Do not set a price on their behalf without their review.
5. **Availability.** Worker sets their own normal hours and available-now setting. Explain that an availability indicator is not a response-time guarantee.
6. **Public media and verification.** Get consent before uploading any photo or portfolio item. Worker enters/submits their own verification reference where required. Record status accurately; “submitted” does not mean approved.
7. **Review and consent.** Let the worker review the public profile on their device. Ask whether they consent to participation, the listed public details, and one pilot follow-up. Record yes/no, date, selected scope, and any requested follow-up without keeping unnecessary personal details.
8. **Close.** Share the worker’s profile link only after their consent and the live launch gate. Explain who checks new requests and when; do not promise automatic alerts if none are configured.

Suggested plain-language consent check (operational record, not legal advice):

> I understand this is an early, small pilot and there may be few or no requests. I reviewed the service, area, price, hours, and public profile details shown here. I agree to participate and to one follow-up about the pilot. I can ask for a correction, pause my availability, or leave by contacting WhatsApp +356 9989 4619 (https://wa.me/35699894619) or markanthonymifsud@yahoo.com. I understand any required verification is separate and is not complete until reviewed.

Record the worker’s affirmative response and the date. Do not ask them to send identity documents by WhatsApp or group chat.

## Illustrative EUR unit economics

These examples help the founder see which assumptions matter. Every number is an assumption, not a current product setting, observed price, cost quote, income forecast, or promise. Actual prices, payment fees, refunds, taxes, support time, and demand are unknown. The live app currently records simulated payments only.

Assumptions for this simple example: average completed booking value **€45**; one payment transaction per completed booking; hypothetical payment-provider cost **2.5% of booking value + €0.25**; hypothetical platform commission of **0%, 8%, or 12%** of booking value. These provider costs are illustrative only and must be replaced with a real provider quote. The model assumes commission revenue is collected successfully and excludes fixed costs, founder time, customer support, refunds/chargebacks, taxes/VAT, insurance, and failed payments.

| Completed bookings | Gross booking value at €45 each | Assumed provider cost | At 0% fee: contribution before excluded costs | At 8% fee: commission less provider cost | At 12% fee: commission less provider cost |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | €450 | €13.75 | −€13.75 | €22.25 | €40.25 |
| 30 | €1,350 | €41.25 | −€41.25 | €66.75 | €120.75 |

This arithmetic is not a recommendation to charge those fees. If the platform pays the payment-processing cost while taking no fee, the example loses that cost before any operating overhead; if workers or customers pay it, the displayed price and applicable rules change. At a hypothetical 8% fee, €45 booking value yields €3.60 gross commission per completed booking before processing and operating costs. A few bookings cannot support a conclusion about financial sustainability. Validate willingness to pay and the payer transparently only after payment, tax, consumer, and accounting readiness; no fee is currently active.
